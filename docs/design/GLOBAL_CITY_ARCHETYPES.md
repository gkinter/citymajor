# IRON & OAK: Global City Archetypes System
## Expanding from Regional Play to a Worldwide Network of Cities

---

## 1. SYSTEM OVERVIEW

Iron & Oak's existing regional play allows 3-5 interconnected towns on a shared map. The Global City Archetypes system expands this to a **worldwide network** where players can found cities in 10 distinct real-world-inspired regions, each with radically different terrain, resources, culture, transport traditions, and challenges.

**Design Philosophy:**
- Every region must feel fundamentally different to play, not just re-skinned
- Regional strengths create hard trade dependencies -- no region is self-sufficient at scale
- Cultural mechanics are not cosmetic; they change simulation math (work hours, social spending, governance costs)
- Transport traditions are baked into starting conditions and unlock trees
- Global trade is the connective tissue that makes the whole system sing

**How This Extends the Existing GDD:**
- The existing era system (1850-2050+) still applies, but each region *enters* eras at different historical dates
- The existing resource/industry system is the foundation; regions get unique subsets and exclusive resources
- The existing transport system provides the base; regions add signature transport modes
- The existing demographic system gains cultural modifiers per region

### 2.1 HEARTLAND (North America)

**Inspiration:** Chicago, Houston, Atlanta -- sprawling grid cities built around the automobile.

**Visual / Pixel Art Direction:**
- Flat grid-based street layouts with wide roads and generous setbacks
- Wooden frontier buildings evolving to brick downtown cores, then glass-and-steel CBD
- Strip malls, parking lots, detached suburban houses with lawns
- Water towers, grain elevators on the outskirts
- Seasonal palette: hot summers (golden-brown grass), snowy winters, vivid autumn foliage
- Neon motel signs and diner aesthetics in the Postwar era

**Terrain & Climate:**
- **Terrain type:** Great Plains -- flat with gentle river valleys, occasional bluffs
- **Grid size modifier:** 1.2x (land is cheap and abundant, bigger buildable area)
- **Climate:** Continental -- hot summers, cold winters, tornado season (spring)
- **Natural disasters:** Tornadoes (destroy buildings in a line), blizzards (halt transport), floods (river valleys)
- **Elevation:** Mostly flat (1 level), river bluffs (2 levels)

**Unique Raw Materials:**
| Resource | Abundance | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Fertile soil | Very High | Breadbasket -- massive agricultural output |
| Oil | High | Shale deposits, offshore Gulf |
| Natural gas | High | Fracking unlocks huge reserves in Modern era |
| Coal | Moderate | Appalachian deposits |
| Iron ore | Moderate | Great Lakes region deposits |
| Timber | Moderate | Northern forests |
| Copper | Low | Limited domestic supply |
| Rare earth | Very Low | Almost none -- must import |
| Lithium | Low | Nevada deposits, limited |

**Exclusive Resource:** Shale Oil (unlocked via fracking tech in Modern era -- massive output but pollution controversy, happiness penalty in educated populations)

**Cultural Mechanics:**

| Mechanic | Value | Effect |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Work ethic | High productivity | +10% industrial output per worker |
| Work hours | Long (45hr/week base) | +12% output, -5% happiness vs. global average |
| Car dependency | Extreme | Citizens refuse transit if car trip < 1.5x transit time (elsewhere: 1.2x) |
| Suburban preference | Strong | Low-density residential demand +30%, high-density -15% |
| Tax tolerance | Low | Citizens emigrate at tax rates 5% lower than global threshold |
| Social spending expectation | Low | Can run leaner public services without happiness penalty |
| Entrepreneurship | High | Commercial zones develop 20% faster |
| Governance style | Minimal regulation | Zoning changes cost 50% less, but pollution rules weaker |
| Immigration attitude | Moderate | Standard immigration rates |

**Transport Traditions:**
- **Starting bonus:** Free 2-lane paved road network (6 tiles of road at game start instead of dirt paths)
- **Signature mode:** Interstate Highway system -- highways cost 20% less to build, +15% capacity
- **Weakness:** Public transit costs 25% more to build (low political will), ridership takes 30% longer to ramp up
- **Unique vehicle:** Yellow school bus (provides education coverage radius like a mobile school building)
- **Parking:** Must zone 1 parking tile per 3 commercial tiles or commercial desirability drops 40% (car-dependent culture)

**Industry Specializations:**
- **Agriculture:** +25% farm output (mechanized farming tradition), exclusive Grain Elevator building (stores 5x normal capacity, enables bulk grain export)
- **Oil & Gas:** +15% extraction efficiency, exclusive Shale Fracking Rig (Modern era)
- **Automotive:** +10% auto factory output, exclusive Big Three Auto Plant (employs 2x workers, produces 2x cars)
- **Tech (late game):** No inherent bonus, but huge university system attracts knowledge workers if funded

**Starting Conditions (Frontier Era, 1850):**
- Population: 200 (40 households)
- Starting cash: $15,000 (above average -- land is cheap)
- Free infrastructure: General store, dirt crossroads, 6 tiles of road, small church
- Starting resources revealed: Fertile soil guaranteed, 1 random mineral deposit
- Unique starting challenge: Railroad company offers to build a rail line through your town -- accept (free rail but they choose the route and it bisects your town) or refuse (build your own later at full cost)

**Unique Challenges:**
- **Sprawl Penalty:** If average residential density stays below 2.0 for 20+ game years, infrastructure maintenance cost per capita increases 30% (roads, pipes, wires stretched thin)
- **Tornado Season:** Each spring, 15% chance of tornado event. Severity scales with era. Must build storm shelters ($500 each, cover 200 residents) or face casualties
- **Oil Bust Cycle:** If oil is primary industry (>30% of GDP), every 15-25 years a price crash halves oil revenue for 3 years

**Signature Buildings (Unlockable):**
| Building | Unlock Condition | Effect |
|----------|-----------------|--------|
| Grain Exchange | Export 10,000 tons of grain | +20% agricultural trade revenue, becomes trade hub |
| Interstate Cloverleaf | Build 20 tiles of highway | Highway capacity +25% in a 10-tile radius |
| National Mall (monument) | Population 100,000 + Democracy governance | +15% tourism, +10% national happiness |
| Space Center | Future era + University + Research Lab | Unlocks satellite industry chain, +30% research |

- **Terrain type:** Coastal mountain -- steep terrain with narrow coastal plain and river valleys
- **Grid size modifier:** 0.7x (smallest buildable area -- mountains and coast constrain heavily)

## 3. PROGRESSIVE CITY UNLOCK SYSTEM

### 3.1 Design Philosophy

Players start with ONE city in a region of their choice. Additional cities in other regions are unlocked by reaching milestones in existing cities. This creates a sense of expanding influence -- your small frontier town grows into a regional power, then a national economy, then a global network.

**Key Principles:**
- First city teaches core mechanics; second city introduces trade; third introduces specialization strategy
- Each subsequent city is harder to manage (attention split, more complex trade networks)
- Unlocking a city in a harder region requires higher milestones
- Players are never forced to expand -- a single maximally-developed city is a valid endgame

### 3.2 Region Difficulty Ratings

Each region has an inherent difficulty rating that affects unlock requirements:

| Region | Difficulty | Rating (1-10) | Why |
|--------|-----------|---------------|-----|
| Heartland (N. America) | Beginner | 3 | Abundant land, resources, forgiving climate |
| Old Continent (W. Europe) | Intermediate | 5 | Constrained land, heritage restrictions, social expectations |
| Rising Sun (E. Asia) | Advanced | 7 | Tiny buildable area, earthquakes, aging crisis |
| Tiger City (SE Asia) | Intermediate | 5 | Flooding challenges, fast growth, corruption |
| Subcontinent (S. Asia) | Advanced | 7 | Extreme density, inequality, slum management |
| Golden Sands (M. East) | Expert | 9 | No water, no agriculture, oil dependency trap, brutal early game |

### 3.4 Difficulty Scaling Formula

Each additional city imposes management overhead:

```
Management Overhead per City = base_overhead * (1 + 0.15 * (total_cities - 1))

Where:
  base_overhead = 1.0 (first city, no penalty)
  City 2: 1.15x management cost (advisors, bureaucracy budget)
  City 3: 1.30x
  City 4: 1.45x
  City 5: 1.60x

Management cost manifests as:
  - 5% slower construction speed per additional city (attention split)
  - 3% higher administrative budget per additional city
  - Events in other cities still trigger and demand response (cannot ignore)
```

**Counterbalancing bonuses for multi-city play:**
- Trade between your own cities has zero tariff (internal trade)
- Research completed in one city applies to all your cities (shared tech tree)
- Surplus population in one city can migrate to another with available jobs (internal migration)
- Shared treasury option: pool money across cities (or keep separate for more control)

### 3.5 New City Founding Process

When founding a new city in a different region:

1. **Choose region** from unlocked regions
2. **Map generation** -- region-specific terrain, climate, and resources generated
3. **Starting conditions** apply per region archetype (see Section 2)
4. **Founding bonus** based on your existing network:
   - If existing city GDP > $100,000/year: new city starts with +$5,000 cash (investment capital)
   - If existing city has international airport: new city starts with +200 population (colonists)
   - If existing city has university: new city starts with +2 RP/month (knowledge transfer)
5. **First trade route** automatically established between new city and nearest existing city (if both have ports/airports/rail connections)

## 4. GLOBAL TRADE SYSTEM

### 4.1 Design Philosophy

Global trade is the connective tissue of the multi-city system. No region can be self-sufficient at high development levels. The game enforces hard dependencies:

- **East Asia** needs oil (from Heartland/Gulf/Russia) and cobalt (from Africa) to make electronics
- **Heartland** needs rare earth (from Africa/Russia) and rubber (from SE Asia) for manufacturing
- **Gulf** needs EVERYTHING except oil -- food, timber, iron, labor
- **Scandinavia** needs oil (from Russia/Gulf) and electronics (from East Asia)
- **Africa** needs electronics (from East Asia), machinery (from Europe/East Asia), and refined petroleum

These hard dependencies create the foundation for meaningful trade.

### 4.2 Trade Good Categories

| Category | Examples | Transport Mode | Value Density | Perishability |
|----------|---------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Bulk Raw** | Iron ore, coal, grain, timber | Ship (cheapest), Rail | Low ($/ton) | None |
| **Liquid Bulk** | Oil, LNG, chemicals | Pipeline, Tanker ship | Medium | None |
| **Manufactured** | Steel, cement, textiles, furniture | Ship, Rail, Truck | Medium | None |
| **High-Value Manufactured** | Electronics, automobiles, machinery | Ship, Air | High | None |
| **Perishable** | Fresh food, flowers, fish | Air, Refrigerated truck | Medium | High (loses value over transit time) |
| **Luxury** | Wine, coffee, gold, jewelry, diamonds | Air, Ship | Very High | None |
| **Digital** | Software, data services, fintech | Fiber optic cable (instant) | Very High | None |
| **Energy** | Electricity (surplus) | Undersea cable, Grid | Medium | Instant (no storage) |

### 4.3 Trade Route Types

#### Shipping Lanes (Bulk & Manufactured Goods)

| Route Type | Capacity | Transit Time | Cost/ton | Establishment Cost | Era |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|-------------------|-----|
| Coastal feeder | 500 tons/month | 3 days | $2/ton | $5,000 (small port) | Industrial |
| Regional shipping | 5,000 tons/month | 7 days | $1.50/ton | $20,000 (commercial port) | Industrial |
| Intercontinental shipping | 50,000 tons/month | 21 days | $0.80/ton | $100,000 (container terminal) | Postwar |
| Mega-container route | 200,000 tons/month | 21 days | $0.40/ton | $500,000 (mega-port) | Modern |

**Shipping lane mechanics:**
- Routes are established between two ports (yours or trade partners')
- Capacity is limited by smallest port on the route
- Transit time affects perishable goods value: `value_on_arrival = base_value * (1 - (transit_days * perishability_rate))`
- Piracy risk in certain sea zones (random event, lose 5-15% of cargo. Reducible with naval escort tech)
- Suez/Panama Canal chokepoints: routes passing through these are cheaper but vulnerable to blockade events

#### Air Freight (High-Value & Perishable)

| Route Type | Capacity | Transit Time | Cost/ton | Requirement | Era |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|-------------|-----|
| Regional air cargo | 50 tons/month | 1 day | $20/ton | Regional airport | Postwar |
| International air cargo | 500 tons/month | 2 days | $12/ton | International airport | Modern |
| Express air freight | 200 tons/month | Same day | $40/ton | International airport + logistics center | Modern |

**Air freight mechanics:**
- Only economical for goods worth > $50/ton (electronics, luxury goods, perishables)
- Airport cargo capacity separate from passenger capacity but shares runway time
- Fuel cost variable: oil price affects operating cost

#### Rail Freight (Overland Continental)

| Route Type | Capacity | Transit Time | Cost/ton | Requirement | Era |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|-------------|-----|
| Standard gauge rail | 2,000 tons/month | 5 days | $1.20/ton | Rail connection between cities | Industrial |
| High-capacity rail | 10,000 tons/month | 3 days | $0.80/ton | Electrified double-track | Postwar |
| Transcontinental rail | 20,000 tons/month | 10 days | $0.60/ton | International rail agreement | Modern |

**Rail freight mechanics:**
- Only works between cities on the same landmass (cannot cross oceans)
- Gauge incompatibility events: some regions use different rail gauges, requiring transfer facilities at borders (+$5,000 cost, +1 day transit time)
- Eastern Europe/Russia uses broad gauge: connecting to Western Europe requires gauge-change facility

#### Pipeline (Oil & Gas Only)

| Route Type | Capacity | Transit Time | Cost/ton | Requirement | Era |
|-----------|----------|-------------|----------|-------------|-----|
| Regional pipeline | 5,000 barrels/month | Instant | $0.30/barrel | Pipeline construction ($50/tile) | Industrial |

| Undersea pipeline | 20,000 barrels/month | Instant | $0.40/barrel | Deep-sea engineering research | Modern |

**Pipeline mechanics:**
- Cheapest way to move oil/gas but enormous upfront investment
- Vulnerable to political disruption events (sanctions, conflicts)
- Maintenance cost: $2/tile/year

#### Digital Trade (Software & Services)

| Route Type | Capacity | Transit Time | Cost | Requirement | Era |
|-----------|----------|-------------|------|-------------|-----|
| Satellite link | Limited bandwidth | Instant | $500/month | Satellite uplink | Modern |

**Digital trade mechanics:**
- Software, fintech, data services trade at zero marginal transport cost
- Revenue = `software_output * global_demand_factor * connectivity_quality`
- Only regions with strong education and tech industries can participate

### 4.4 Trade Agreement System

Trade between cities requires agreements. These work differently in single-player vs. multiplayer.

**Agreement Types:**

| Agreement | Effect | Cost to Establish | Duration |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|----------|
| **Basic Trade** | Can buy/sell on open market at market price | Free | Permanent |
| **Bilateral Trade Deal** | -10% tariff between two specific cities | Diplomatic effort (3 months) | 10 years, renewable |
| **Free Trade Zone** | Zero tariff between 3+ cities | Major diplomatic effort (1 year) | 20 years |
| **Exclusive Supply Contract** | Guaranteed purchase of X units/month at fixed price | Negotiation + premium price | 5 years |
| **Embargo** | Block all trade with a target city/region | Diplomatic action | Until lifted |

**Tariff System:**
```
Base tariff = 15% of goods value
Modified by:
  + Trade agreement modifier (-10% to -15%)
  + Political relationship modifier (-5% friendly to +10% hostile)
  + Retaliatory tariff (if partner imposed tariffs on you)
  + Era modifier (tariffs decrease in Modern/Future era by -3% per era)

Minimum tariff = 0% (free trade zone)
Maximum tariff = 40% (trade war)

Tariff revenue goes to importing city's treasury.
```

### 4.5 Global Market Price System

Each trade good has a global market price that fluctuates based on supply and demand:

```
Price Calculation (per game-month):
  global_supply = sum of all cities' production of this good
  global_demand = sum of all cities' consumption need for this good

  supply_demand_ratio = global_supply / global_demand

  price = base_price * (1 / supply_demand_ratio) ^ elasticity

  Where elasticity varies by good:
    Essential goods (food, fuel): elasticity = 0.3 (price swings huge when supply is short)
    Standard goods (steel, textiles): elasticity = 0.5
    Luxury goods (wine, gold): elasticity = 0.8 (price less volatile)
    Digital goods (software): elasticity = 1.0 (price tracks demand closely)
```

**Price Events:**
- **Oil shock:** If major oil-producing city/region disrupted, oil price spikes 3x for 1-3 years
- **Commodity crash:** If new supply source comes online (e.g., shale oil), prices crash 50% for producers
- **Tech boom:** Electronics demand surges 2x when any region enters Future era
- **Food crisis:** If global food supply drops below 90% of demand, food prices spike 4x

## 5. MULTIPLAYER SCALING

### 5.1 Multiplayer Architecture

**Model:** Persistent world server with asynchronous play (like Civilization: Beyond Earth's async multiplayer or Offworld Trading Company).

**Player Count:** 2-20 players per world server.

**World Structure:**
- One shared global map with all 10 regions
- Each region can support 1-3 cities (10-30 total city slots)
- Players claim city slots when founding new cities
- Multiple players CAN have cities in the same region (competing for same local resources)

### 5.2 How Players Interact

| Interaction Type | Mechanism | Competitive/Cooperative |
|-----------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Trade** | Buy/sell goods on global market or bilateral deals | Cooperative |
| **Resource competition** | Two cities in same region compete for shared mineral deposits | Competitive |
| **Labor competition** | Cities compete for migrants based on attractiveness | Competitive |
| **Trade agreements** | Negotiate bilateral deals, free trade zones | Cooperative |
| **Embargoes** | Block trade to punish or pressure another player | Competitive |
| **Price manipulation** | Flood market with cheap goods to crash prices (hurt competitors) | Competitive |
| **Research sharing** | Trade tech with allied players | Cooperative |
| **Joint infrastructure** | Co-fund transcontinental rail or undersea cable | Cooperative |
| **Global events** | All players affected by pandemics, oil shocks, etc. | Shared challenge |

### 5.3 Global Leaderboard & Victory Conditions

There is no single "win" condition. Instead, a multi-dimensional leaderboard tracks different measures of success:

| Leaderboard Category | Metric | Playstyle It Rewards |
|---------------------|--------|---------------------|
| **Population King** | Total population across all cities | Growth-focused |
| **Economic Titan** | Total GDP across all cities | Efficiency-focused |
| **Trade Emperor** | Total trade volume (imports + exports) | Trade-focused |
| **Quality of Life** | Average happiness across all cities (weighted by population) | Citizen-focused |
| **Green Pioneer** | Lowest carbon emissions per capita | Sustainability-focused |
| **Tech Visionary** | Total research points generated | Research-focused |
| **Cultural Icon** | Total signature buildings + tourism revenue | Prestige-focused |
| **Transit Master** | Highest transit mode share + lowest congestion | Infrastructure-focused |
| **Global Connector** | Most active trade routes with other players | Diplomacy-focused |
| **Composite Score** | Weighted average of all above | Balanced play |

**Composite Score Formula:**
```
composite = (population_rank * 0.10)
          + (gdp_rank * 0.15)
          + (trade_rank * 0.15)
          + (happiness_rank * 0.15)
          + (green_rank * 0.10)
          + (research_rank * 0.10)
          + (cultural_rank * 0.10)
          + (transit_rank * 0.05)
          + (connector_rank * 0.10)

Where each rank is normalized to 0-100 scale (100 = best player in world)
```

### 5.4 Multiplayer-Specific Mechanics

#### 5.4.1 The Global Market (Shared)

All players trade on a single global market. Prices are determined by aggregate supply and demand (see Section 4.5). This means:

- If you are the only player producing cobalt (Africa), you have enormous pricing power
- If 5 players all produce oil, oil prices crash and all oil-dependent economies suffer
- A player can intentionally dump cheap goods to undercut a competitor's exports
- Cartels: two or more players can informally agree to limit production to keep prices high (no enforcement mechanism -- trust only)

#### 5.4.2 Diplomatic Relations

Each player pair has a **relationship score** (-100 to +100):

```
Starting relationship: 0 (neutral)

Positive actions:
  + Bilateral trade deal: +10
  + Honoring trade contracts: +2/year
  + Joint infrastructure project: +15
  + Sharing research: +5 per tech shared
  + Sending aid during disaster: +20

Negative actions:
  - Imposing embargo: -30
  - Breaking trade contract: -25
  - Price dumping (identified): -15
  - Poaching migrants (advertising in their city): -10
  - Refusing aid request: -5

Relationship effects:
  Score > 50: Trade bonus (-5% tariff), migration sharing, research collaboration
  Score 0-50: Standard trade, neutral
  Score -50 to 0: Trade friction (+5% tariff), reduced migration
  Score < -50: Trade war risk, embargo possible, migrant exodus from hostile city
```

#### 5.4.3 Global Council (Late-Game Cooperative)

When 3+ players reach Modern era, a **Global Council** forms. Players can propose and vote on global policies:

| Policy | Effect | Vote Threshold |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| **Carbon Tax** | All cities pay $X per unit of pollution | 60% majority |
| **Free Trade Accord** | All tariffs reduced by 10% globally | 75% majority |
| **Pandemic Preparedness Fund** | All cities contribute 2% of GDP; pandemic events are shorter | 60% majority |
| **Technology Sharing Protocol** | All researched techs become 50% cheaper for all players | Unanimous |
| **Maritime Safety Pact** | Piracy events eliminated; each player contributes to naval fund | 60% majority |
| **Anti-Dumping Regulation** | Price dumping penalties enforced automatically | 75% majority |

Voting power is weighted by GDP (larger economies have more votes, but cannot unilaterally pass anything).

#### 5.4.4 Asynchronous Play Model

Not all players are online simultaneously. The game handles this:

- **Game time advances in real-time ticks** (1 game-month = 1 real-hour, configurable per server)
- **Offline cities continue simulating** on autopilot using player-set policies (tax rates, trade priorities, construction queues)
- **Trade offers and diplomatic proposals** are queued; offline players have 24 real-hours to respond before auto-decisions based on their policy presets
- **Emergency events** (disasters, market crashes) are handled by AI advisor if player is offline, following player-set priority rules
- **Speed settings per server:** Casual (1 game-month = 4 hours), Standard (1 month = 1 hour), Competitive (1 month = 15 minutes)

#### 5.4.5 Anti-Griefing Protections

| Grief Vector | Protection |
|-------------|-----------|
| Embargo spam | Embargo requires relationship < -30 AND costs the embargoing player 5% GDP |
| Market manipulation | Anti-dumping regulation available via Global Council vote |
| Resource hoarding | If a player controls >40% of a critical resource and withholds it, emergency UN bypass allows others to import at 2x price from AI neutral markets |
| Abandonment | If a player goes offline for 7+ real days, their cities are managed by AI. After 30 days, cities become AI-controlled neutral trade partners |
| New player bullying | New cities have a 30-day "founding protection" -- no embargoes, guaranteed basic trade access |

### 5.5 Single-Player Global Mode

For players who prefer solo play, the global system works with AI-controlled cities:

- **8-12 AI cities** populate the world across different regions
- AI cities grow and trade according to their regional archetype strengths
- AI difficulty levels: Passive (trades freely, low competition), Standard (competes for markets), Aggressive (tries to dominate trade, uses embargoes)
- Player can choose how many AI cities exist and their difficulty
- All trade, diplomatic, and Global Council mechanics work identically
- AI governors have personality types: Industrialist (heavy industry focus), Green Pioneer (renewables focus), Trade Baron (maximizes trade), Populist (maximizes happiness)

## 8. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

### 8.1 Integration with Existing GDD Systems

This global archetypes system builds on top of every existing Iron & Oak system:

- **Era System (GDD Section 3.3):** Each region uses the same 5 eras but enters them at different historical dates. Rising Sun and Old Continent start at Frontier but may reach Industrial faster (earlier industrialization historically). The era unlock milestones are the same, but cultural modifiers affect speed.

- **Transport System (GDD Section 4):** All existing transport modes (roads, rail, bus, tram, metro, water, air) remain. Regions add exclusive modes (tuk-tuk, matatu, bullet train, etc.) and modify costs/effectiveness of standard modes.

- **Industry System (GDD Section 5):** All 20 existing production chains remain. Regions add exclusive chains (wine, rubber, coffee, etc.) and modify efficiency of standard chains.

- **Demographics (GDD Section 6):** The household simulation is identical. Cultural mechanics modify the parameters (birth rate, migration rates, wealth distribution, education demand).

- **Research Tree (Research Doc):** The tech tree is shared globally. Researching a tech in one city unlocks it for all your cities. Some techs have region-specific variants (e.g., "Earthquake Engineering" only appears in Rising Sun's tree).

### 8.2 Performance Considerations

- Each city runs its own simulation instance
- Global market calculations run once per game-month (not per tick)
- Trade route pathfinding is cached and only recalculated when routes change
- AI cities in single-player use simplified simulation (lower tick rate)
- Maximum 5 player cities + 12 AI cities = 17 simultaneous simulations
- Multiplayer sync happens at month boundaries, not continuously

### 8.3 Art Asset Requirements Per Region

Each region requires:
- 60-80 unique building sprites (across 5 eras, multiple density levels)
- 10-15 unique terrain tiles (ground, water, vegetation)
- 5-8 unique vehicle sprites (signature transport modes)
- 3-5 signature building sprites (detailed, landmark-quality)
- 1 unique UI skin/color palette for the city management panels
- Season-specific palette swaps for all above
- Ambient sound set (traffic sounds, nature sounds, cultural music snippets)

**Estimated total:** ~800 unique sprites per region, ~8,000 total across all 10 regions. This is a major art investment but essential for each region feeling distinct.

### 8.4 Content Unlock Roadmap

Not all 10 regions need to ship at once. Suggested release order:

| Release | Regions | Rationale |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Launch (v1.0)** | Heartland, Old Continent, Rising Sun | Most familiar to core audience, covers 3 continents, strong trade triangle |
| **Expansion 1** | Tiger City, Frost Crown | Adds tropical + Nordic contrast, rubber trade dependency |
| **Expansion 2** | Subcontinent, Golden Sands | Adds extreme density + extreme desert, oil trade |
| **Expansion 3** | Savanna Rising, Amazonia | Adds critical minerals + lithium, completes resource web |
| **Expansion 4** | Iron Curtain | Adds final region, gas pipeline politics, completes the globe |

Each expansion adds not just regions but new global trade dynamics that change the entire game economy.
