## Key Strategic Takeaways

**1. You occupy an genuinely underserved niche.** Deep simulation + pixel art is nearly empty. 3D dominates simulation, pixel art dominates charm. The intersection has almost no competition. This is your strongest advantage.

**2. Price at $24.99 EA, raise to $29.99 at 1.0.** Pixel art historically commands lower prices than 3D, but the simulation depth justifies premium indie pricing. The $24.99 sweet spot converts well on sales (20% off = $19.99 impulse buy).

**3. Target 7,000--10,000 wishlists before EA launch.** This triggers Steam's "Popular Upcoming" list and gives you enough first-week sales (~1,500--2,000) to potentially hit "New & Trending." At $24.99, that is $26K--$35K gross in Week 1 after Steam's cut.

**4. Steam Next Fest is a multiplier, not a miracle.** You roughly double your existing wishlists. Enter with 2,000+ minimum. Release your demo months before the festival, not at it.

**5. Plan for 2--3 years of EA.** City builders average 3--5 years in EA, but as a solo dev with AI assistants, 2--3 years is realistic. Free updates only during EA to build trust and reviews.

**6. Avoid Season Passes; sell individual DLC post-1.0.** Maximum flexibility as a solo dev. First paid expansion ($15) at ~6 months after 1.0. Free QoL patches alongside every paid release.

**7. Revenue expectations should be grounded.** A moderate scenario is 25,000 lifetime units = ~$407K net. Pessimistic is 5,000 units = ~$81K. The genre has excellent long tails -- Settlement Survival was acquired for $10.7M years after release.

**8. Demo strategy is now critical.** Valve's July 2024 demo update makes demos behave like free games with their own store pages and algorithmic visibility. Release a demo and keep it up.

**9. Medieval fatigue is real -- differentiate.** 444 city builders released on Steam in 2025, with medieval colony sims being the most saturated subgenre. If Iron & Oak is medieval, lean hard on the era progression mechanic or unique social simulation to stand out.

**10. Solve the late-game flatline.** The #1 player complaint across the genre. Plan endgame systems (politics, cultural evolution, crises, era progression) from the beginning, not as an afterthought.
