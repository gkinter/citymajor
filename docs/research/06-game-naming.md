## Key Findings

### 1. What Makes a Great Game Title

The research converges on a clear set of rules from Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game), GDC talks, and analysis of 90,000+ Steam titles:

- **1-3 words** is the sweet spot for Steam capsule art and YouTube thumbnails
- **The "Crowded Bar" test**: can someone hear it, spell it, and type it into Steam on the first try?
- **Genre signal is non-negotiable**: city builder fans look for words like City, Settlement, Builder, Tycoon, Metropolis, -polis, -scape
- **Searchability beats cleverness**: a "boring" descriptive name outsells a "cool" confusing one in the simulation genre
- **Syllable flow**: 2-4 syllables in the primary title (Frost-punk, Sim-City, Man-or Lords)
- **The "Colon Hack"**: artistic name + genre subtitle is a growing proven trend

### 2. Competitor Title Patterns

Four naming frameworks dominate successful city builders:

| Pattern | Examples | Best for |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Literal | SimCity, Cities: Skylines | Maximum genre clarity |
| Portmanteau | Frostpunk, Timberborn | Unique + Googleable + genre-clear |
| Role/Status | Manor Lords, Tropico | Games with player identity/narrative |
| Action + Struggle | Against the Storm | Survival/roguelite city builders |

**Critical competitor**: Metropolis 1998 -- your closest aesthetic rival (retro pixel art + deep simulation). Solo dev, pre-launch viral success. Uses the "Setting + Era Number" format. Your name must differentiate clearly.

### 3. Evaluation of Current Candidates

**"Iron & Oak" -- NOT RECOMMENDED (23/35)**
- Two board games with nearly identical names create search pollution
- Does NOT signal "city builder" -- reads as crafting/survival game
- Ampersand is harder to search
- Beautiful thematically, but fails the practical tests

**"CityMajor" -- RECOMMENDED (27/35)**
- "City" immediately signals genre -- zero ambiguity
- Unique compound word, will dominate search results instantly
- Passes every practical test: spellable, pronounceable, typeable
- Weakness: doesn't signal era-spanning or depth (fixable with subtitle)

### 4. The Strongest Alternative: "Brickborn" (28/35)

Follows the exact Frostpunk/Timberborn naming pattern. "Brick" = building/city, "Born" = origins/growth. Zero search conflicts. Subtle pixel art resonance (bricks = pixels). 2 syllables, punchy, memorable. Could become iconic.

Full list with scoring is in the plan document. The top tier:

### 6. Strategic Recommendation

**Go with "CityMajor"** as the primary title and address its weaknesses with:

- A subtitle for depth/era signal: **"CityMajor: Ages of Industry"** or **"CityMajor: A City Through Time"**
- Capsule art showing era progression (1850s to future)
- Steam tags emphasizing "deep simulation", "historical", "5 eras", "pixel art"
- Marketing tagline: *"Build a city. Shape an era. Write history."*

If you want something bolder with higher ceiling but higher risk, **"Brickborn"** follows the exact naming DNA of the genre's biggest modern hits.
